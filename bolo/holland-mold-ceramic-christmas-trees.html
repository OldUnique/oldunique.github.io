VINTAGE CERAMIC HOLIDAY DÉCOR · PRICES CHECKED OCTOBER 5, 2026

Holland Mold ceramic Christmas trees: size and completeness set the ceiling

Conservative price signal: Six comparable 10–13 inch trees averaged $66.05 before shipping. Larger musical examples can sell much higher, but the ordinary complete tabletop tree is the safer buying lane.

Sample: 6 pre-owned 10–13 inch Holland Mold tree sales from Sep. 27–Oct. 4, 2026; bases-only, tops-only, missing-light projects, modern reproductions and 17–21 inch premium examples excluded.

Exact identification cues

Look for “Holland Mold” impressed or incised into the ceramic base or underside, often with a handwritten decorator name or year. The classic form is a glazed green two-piece tree with removable plastic light pegs, a hollow base and an interior bulb. Measure total assembled height; 10–13 inches is the price group used here.

Condition and testing

Confirm the tree, base, electrical socket, cord and most or all plastic pegs are present. Test the light for several minutes, inspect every branch tip and rim for hairlines or repairs, and check that the top sits securely on the base. Replaced pegs are acceptable when disclosed; chips and glued branches are not.

Buy and listing plan

Try to pay no more than: $20–$25 complete and working
Suggested listing price: $79.99–$99.99 for a clean 10–13 inch example
Quick-sale target: $59.99–$69.99

Shipping plan

Remove loose pegs and bag them separately. Wrap the tree and base as separate fragile pieces with generous cushioning, immobilize both inside a sturdy box, and double-box valuable examples. Never let the ceramic tree carry the base’s weight in transit.

Main risks

Height changes value quickly, and large musical trees are not comparable to ordinary tabletop pieces. Missing bases, cords, bulbs or many pegs reduce value; undisclosed repairs create return risk. Shipping breakage is the biggest operational risk.
